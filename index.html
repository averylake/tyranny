01 / The Work

A figure stands behind the alphabet.

Columns of letters fall in straight lines across a dark plate. A human form stands behind them. The letters are bars, and the body is on the far side of the bars.

The circles are ideas. They rise through the figure, gather, and press upward against the ruled order that holds them. Some reach the edge and begin to push past it. None have left.

The letters flicker where they stand. The grid holds, and it is not as solid as it looks. The loop closes without a cut, so the condition continues: not a moment of capture, but a state that has been running so long we mistake it for the shape of thought itself.

02 / The Alphabet

The first capture technology.

Thought, before writing, is simultaneous, associative, and bodily. Phonetic writing takes it and makes it run in lines. This is not a loss to be mourned or a gain to be celebrated; it is a restructuring, and it is the one we have stopped being able to see.

01

c. 1050 BCE

The consonantal alphabet

The Phoenician script reduces language to roughly two dozen signs. Writing stops being the property of a scribal class and becomes portable, teachable, and fast.

02

c. 800 BCE

Vowels, and the sealed line

The Greek adaptation adds vowels and completes the phonetic mapping. Eric Havelock argued that this is where a fully sequential, analytic mode of thought becomes possible, and where the oral world begins to close.

Havelock, Preface to Plato ↗
03

1440s

Movable type

The line is mechanised and multiplied. Marshall McLuhan read print as the moment the visual, sequential bias of the alphabet becomes the organising logic of an entire culture.

McLuhan, The Gutenberg Galaxy ↗
04

1870s

QWERTY

The columns in this work read in the order a typewriter gave us. It was an engineering compromise inside one machine in the 1870s, and it is still the arrangement under nearly every hand that writes.

Britannica ↗
05

2026

The fluent grid

A large language model is not a departure from the alphabet. It is the most fluent thing ever built out of it: a system that predicts the next character in the line, at a scale no scribe could hold.

Walter Ong called writing a technology that restructures thought. The question this work asks is not whether that is true, but how a person is supposed to notice a structure they think inside of.

03 / Lineage

One grid inside a longer argument.

Avery Lake's work returns to a single claim: every dominant medium becomes a mirror, and every mirror decides in advance what can be reflected. On the Tyranny of Alphabetic Linearity is the case of the alphabet itself, which is the mirror we stopped noticing because we think through it.

04 / Provenance

A computed master, held as one.

The work exists as a ten-second silent video master of 140 computed frames. Its first and final states are continuous, so the cycle repeats without a visible seam. It is held as a unique work.
